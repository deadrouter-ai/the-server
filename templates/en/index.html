Articles of Common Inquiry

What separates this from OpenRouter? (DeadRouter vs. OpenRouter)

OpenRouter operates with a corporate charter: they offer a vast catalog of 900+ models and a polished UI built to satisfy enterprise boards. However, their operations remain anchored to logging and surveillance — a tax on your privacy levied without your consent.

DeadRouter holds a different principle: we curate only the most private, secure, and freedom-respecting compute providers. Some exist on mainstream networks; others are sovereign nodes you will find nowhere else. OpenRouter serves corporate compliance; we serve individual liberty.

Why is your list of AI models smaller than the competition?

We do not believe in quantity over principle. While other gateways boast hundreds of models, most are built atop infrastructure that harvests your prompts for future training — a silent toll on your liberty.

We curate our roster with extreme care, offering only models run on verified confidential infrastructure or those with native Zero Data Retention (ZDR) and Zero Data Scanning (ZDS) guarantees. We field only the most capable and trustworthy agents.

ZDR vs. ZDS: A gentleman's promise or an iron lock?

We pioneered the concept of Zero Data Scanning (ZDS) because Zero Data Retention (ZDR) has become a corporate loophole. Under standard ZDR, a provider promises to delete your prompt after generation — but they still scan, classify, and inspect your words in transit. The tyrant reads the letter before burning it.

Platforms like OpenRouter explicitly reserve the right to scan and categorize incoming queries. Even if they promise not to link classification logs to your account, that scan still occurs — and their "unlinkability" claims can collapse instantly under legal pressure. Major providers like OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google actively deploy automated safety classifiers that can forward your IP and prompt content to authorities based solely on their own internal judgment, without a court order. ZDS removes this vulnerability entirely by ensuring the provider is blinded in transit or operates within verifiable hardware enclaves — making surveillance mathematically impossible.

What are these AMD SEV-SNP "Confidential Enclaves" you speak of?

Our gateway runs inside AMD SEV-SNP hardware enclaves. Your requests are processed in cryptographically isolated memory regions — a fortified keep inside the server itself.

Even if the physical server is seized, or the hosting provider attempts to dump the RAM, the CPU hardware encrypts memory with a key generated entirely on-chip. No hypervisor, no root user, no foreign magistrate can read the plaintext data flowing through our enclaves. This is not a policy — it is mathematics.

In which sovereign territories are your servers located?

We choose our hosting with extreme deliberation. Our primary physical nodes and legal anchor reside within the borders of the Republic of Chile. While others flock to Iceland or Switzerland, Chile stands as a formidable sovereign fortress. When the exact legal statutes and their real-world enforcement are examined, Chile proves far more resilient against foreign legal pressure than mainstream alternatives.

We are partnered with a rare local provider possessing hardware capable of supporting AMD SEV-SNP — a capability nearly impossible to find outside the major cloud empires. We wholly reject AWS, Google Cloud, and Azure, which are notorious for silent compliance with national security demands.

How strong is the privacy shield protecting my queries?

We draw a clear, inviolable line between true anonymity and convenience, offering two distinct tiers:

  • Zero Logs Accounts (Maximum Liberty): Forged with an opaque, random identifier. All account state (except for the unlinked balance token) is client-side or securely encrypted. In this mode you must pay exclusively with cryptocurrency. We never log transactions or query history. Once a response is returned, our database retains no record of the exchange. We cannot mathematically prove a specific request ever occurred.
  • Standard Accounts (Fiat-Enabled): For those who still require card payments. Due to strict anti-fraud rules imposed by card networks, we must retain transaction metadata. We do not save your prompt text, but we do log the timestamp, model used, tokens consumed, balance changes, and account links. If authorities demand this metadata, we can and will release it. We never collect IP addresses or device fingerprints.

If your work demands full liberty, pay with cryptocurrency and register with no personal information.

What happens when I delete my account?

Deleting a Zero Logs Account initiates an immediate erasure: your API keys and login tokens are invalidated at once (with a 7-day automated backup recovery window before permanent deletion).

For a Standard Account, the metadata of your card transactions must be retained for 90 days to satisfy card network dispute-prevention requirements. If you value complete and instant dissolution, use Zero Logs accounts funded by cryptocurrency.

We offer no corporate SLAs. Running bleeding-edge AMD SEV-SNP hardware out of a sovereign citadel in Chile means occasionally weathering earthly disruptions. We work diligently to maintain full availability, but unforeseen events occur.

Check our Tor status page, and remember: a brief interruption of service is vastly preferable to perpetual surveillance.

Will the AI judge what I ask it?

We are the courier, not the censor. What you write remains between you and the AI you addressed it to. DeadRouter enforces no editorial policy — only mathematical security guarantees.

Because we natively support Zero Data Scanning (ZDS), we are literally incapable of inspecting your content — all requests are processed blindly within the Trusted Execution Environments (TEE) of our servers. If a model refuses your query, that is the model's own alignment policy at work. In such cases, consider pivoting to one of the fine-tuned, uncensored models we host, or crafting a more persuasive argument — as any free citizen should.
